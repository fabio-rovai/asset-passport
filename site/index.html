Asset Passport

This autumn people started merging games with AI: Minecraft inside Elden Ring, Call of Duty with skateboarding. Nothing checks that a sword still means a sword on the other side. Here are two small games of our own and a border between them. An item that crosses carries a passport, and the receiving game checks it itself. Lean re-runs the proof of what the item is, and the game's own contract decides what it may do there.

Smuggle the bed into Ashen

Ashen refuses respawn anchors: death sends you back to the last bonfire, and an item that moves that point breaks the game's loop. Five forgers tried to get Blocky's bed across anyway. Each attempt below was run against the real engine when this page was built.

How the border works

Each game publishes a profile: its units, its health scale, and a mapping of its own item words into one shared Game Asset ontology. A Blocky Bed is a ga:RespawnAnchor. An Ashen Greatsword is a ga:TwoHandedWeapon. With one hub, twenty games need twenty mappings instead of one hundred and ninety pairwise ones.

Classify

The sending game's engine reasons over the item with OWL 2 RL and writes a certificate of every step. The receiving game runs its own copy of a Lean checker over that certificate, whose soundness is a machine-checked theorem.

Convert

The receiving game declares how foreign numbers become its own. Here both declare the same policy: damage and healing keep their share of a player's health, length goes through metres, and armour keeps its share of damage removed. This is a policy, not a fact. A game that wants absolute damage writes a different rule. The rule ships in every passport, so anyone can run it again.

Admit

The receiving game's border is a SHACL contract. It runs twice, on Open Ontologies' SPARQL-compiling evaluator and on its verified evaluator, and an item crosses only when both agree. Losses are warnings: the item enters, and its passport names what had nowhere to go.
